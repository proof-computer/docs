# Liskov legal suite — change log

:::caution[Not in force]
Review version 1.0, dated 3 September 2026. This change log accompanies review
material that has no contractual effect and is excluded from the production
documentation build. Publication blockers remain open.
:::

**Review date:** 3 September 2026

This log records the material changes from the uploaded draft pack to the clean replacement suite. It is thematic rather than a Word redline. The original upload and extracted originals are retained in the bundle for counsel comparison.

## 1. Structural changes

| Area | Draft risk | Replacement approach |
|---|---|---|
| Service model | Hosted SaaS, decentralised infrastructure and customer crypto activity were insufficiently separated. | Defined PROOF-controlled Services, independent Network Infrastructure/Participants and customer-controlled wallets/transactions as distinct layers. |
| Customer type | Consumer exposure was not consistently excluded. | Business-only eligibility and authority representation, with no self-service or manual consumer exception. Product/Legal approved the exact account-creation wording and strict response on 4 September 2026. |
| Contract hierarchy | Orders, policies, privacy/DPA and product statements could conflict. | Express precedence: Order; DPA for data matters; SLA/Security Schedule; Master Terms; Credits Policy; Policies. |
| Marketplace | Draft language anticipated a marketplace without resolving seller, payments, tax and consumer roles. | Free-only curated catalogue; no buyer payment, publisher payout, commission, transferable Credits or agency. Paid Marketplace reserved for separate project. |
| Data protection | A conventional DPA risked implying compliant processing through anonymous/dynamic nodes. | Standard Service prohibits Personal Data in Distributed Workloads; DPA limited to PROOF-controlled hosted processing; enterprise exception requires named/approved chain. |
| Payments/credits | Labels risked obscuring e-money/payment/cryptoasset substance and refund treatment. | Closed-loop, non-transferable contractual prepayment; PROOF acts as principal; no trust/custody/exchange; limited fair refund rules; insolvency disclosure. |
| Platform safety | No operational notice-and-action framework. | Added reporting, triage, emergency action, reasons, counter-notice, appeal, repeat-abuse and recordkeeping policy. |
| Legal horizon | Cyber-regulatory perimeter not operationalised. | Standing NIS/cyber-resilience assessment and re-review triggers. |

## 2. Master Business Terms

- Added complete definitions for Applications, Distributed Workloads/Data, Network Infrastructure/Participants, Launch/Launch Fee, Service Credits, Customer Data and Marketplace.
- Added B2B-only eligibility and authority language; on 4 September 2026,
  Product/Legal approved the versioned account-creation statement, business-
  establishment country declaration and strict no-consumer-exception response.
- Added clickwrap formation, document incorporation and purchase-order exclusion.
- Added service-change and discontinuation remedies rather than an unrestricted unilateral variation right.
- Clarified support targets versus binding SLAs and beta status.
- Added detailed decentralised-network risk allocation without excluding PROOF’s own negligence/breach.
- Stated that PROOF procures network capacity/tokens as principal by default and customers have no proprietary interest in PROOF wallets/tokens.
- Added customer-wallet/private-key responsibility and no-custody position.
- Added strict data classification and Standard Service prohibition on Personal Data, credentials and location-restricted data in workloads.
- Narrowed PROOF’s licence to Customer Content and expressly excluded general-model training without separate agreement.
- Separated PROOF Controller activity from Processor activity and addressed public blockchains.
- Added shared-responsibility security language.
- Made Marketplace free-only and subject to separate terms.
- Added variable Network Cost estimates, spending controls, billing disputes and late-payment process.
- Added Credits and cryptoasset-payment clauses consistent with the standalone Policy.
- Balanced confidentiality, suspension, notice and appeal mechanics.
- Aligned termination export and deletion: 30-day export window; active deletion by day 60; backup overwrite by day 90.
- Added reasonable-skill-and-care remedy and avoided an absolute “as is” disclaimer for paid Services.
- Added reciprocal, controlled indemnity procedure and a limited PROOF IP indemnity.
- Rebuilt liability into non-excludable, enhanced and general cap buckets; preserved direct restoration/incident costs within the cap.
- Added sanctions, export-control, anti-bribery and regulatory-change provisions.
- Added material-change notice and termination/refund right.
- Added complete boilerplate: force majeure, assignment, subcontracting, legal notices, entire agreement, severance, third-party rights, electronic signature, English law/courts.

## 3. Service Credits and Payments Policy

- Defined paid, promotional, service-adjustment and committed Credits.
- Prohibited transfers, trading, pledging, third-party payments and withdrawals.
- Disclosed no trust, safeguarding, interest or specific-asset ownership.
- Added final-settlement requirement and correction of reversals/errors.
- Added token contract/network verification, confirmation/finality and unsupported-transfer process.
- Added depeg, fork, issuer, exploit and sanctions pause rights.
- Clarified merchant acceptance and prohibited exchange/custody/investment features.
- Added consumption order, estimates, spending caps and negative-balance handling.
- Added explicit auto-top-up configuration and receipts.
- Prevented routine expiry of paid Credits while active; kept promotional expiry transparent.
- Replaced blanket forfeiture with defined closure/discontinuation/breach refunds, deductions, verification and timing.
- Added chargeback, tax, ledger and record provisions.

## 4. Acceptable Use Policy

- Added Standard Service data restrictions.
- Distinguished authorised security research from attack.
- Added malware, phishing, botnet, DDoS, secret theft, spam, proxy/relay and resource-abuse rules.
- Added child-safety, terrorism, threat, exploitation and illegal-content provisions.
- Added IP, confidentiality, open-source and model-training restrictions.
- Added blockchain theft/manipulation, regulated cryptoasset activity, mixer and hidden-mining controls.
- Added sanctions/export-control and unknown-node-geography warning.
- Added weapons, surveillance and high-impact/regulated-use restrictions.
- Added Marketplace integrity rules.
- Added proportionate investigation, graduated enforcement, reasons, appeal and restoration.

## 5. Privacy Notice

- Clarified Controller/Processor role split and workload Personal Data prohibition.
- Added explicit public-blockchain immutability and data-minimisation warning.
- Expanded data categories to cover wallet addresses, transaction hashes, Credits, security and Marketplace moderation.
- Added sources, purposes, lawful bases and legitimate-interest context.
- Added fraud/security automation and human-review safeguards.
- Added recipients and transfer mechanisms.
- Added a retention schedule aligned with contract deletion timing.
- Added data-protection rights, complaints process and Data (Use and Access) Act commencement check.
- Added children, marketing, cookies and third-party-service sections.
- Marked all factual fields requiring completion rather than inventing them.

## 6. Data Processing Addendum

- Limited scope to Customer Personal Data processed in PROOF-controlled hosted Services.
- Expressly excluded Standard Service Distributed Workloads and public-chain records.
- Added instructions, unlawful-instruction procedure and controller warranty.
- Added security, breach, rights, DPIA and regulator-assistance clauses.
- Added Subprocessor authorisation, notice, objection and remedy.
- Added UK IDTA/Addendum and transfer-risk framework.
- Added evidence-first audit model with proportionate onsite right.
- Aligned export/deletion dates with Master Terms.
- Added detailed Processing Annex, factual technical-measures checklist and transfer-instrument Annex.

## 7. Cookie Notice

- Reframed as a technology register, not a generic assurance.
- Added strict-necessity, functionality, analytics and advertising categories.
- Required prior blocking, reject parity, granular choices, consent evidence and withdrawal.
- Added Data (Use and Access) Act exception verification.
- Added production scan scope, analytics minimisation and release test.

## 8. Marketplace Terms

- Restricted launch to free Items and no payments/payouts.
- Clarified Item Licence controls user rights and open-source licences prevail.
- Added publisher business status, verification and account security.
- Added complete submission manifest and publisher warranties.
- Narrowed PROOF’s hosting/scanning/display licence.
- Added automated checks without security guarantee.
- Added transparent principal ranking factors and no paid placement at launch.
- Added user due diligence, sandbox testing, least privilege and output validation.
- Added maintenance/update/end-of-life rules.

- Added rights-report, reasons, remedy and appeal process.

## 9. Notice-and-Action Policy

New document covering:

- single reporting routes and safe child/security reporting;
- legal, safety, IP, privacy, sanctions and policy grounds;
- notice requirements and special IP details;
- emergency/high/standard triage;
- proportionate actions and evidence preservation;
- publisher notice, counter-notice and appeal;
- repeat abuse and false notices;
- reporter confidentiality and law-enforcement verification;
- transparency and decision records.

## 10. Matters deliberately not papered over

The clean suite does not claim that:

- calling a product “non-custodial” determines FCA treatment;
- calling value “Credits” prevents payment/e-money regulation;
- open node operators are automatically independent controllers rather than part of a processing chain;
- encryption automatically makes workload data anonymous;
- pre-moderation necessarily removes Online Safety Act scope;
- an English governing-law clause removes mandatory overseas law;
- a scanner, badge or TEE guarantees Marketplace/node security;
- a privacy notice can substitute for actual transfer contracts or cookie consent;
- a liability exclusion cures an unsupported product promise.

Those questions are left as explicit launch gates.
